# Harvest interview — core-analytics pilot query

> **Mock session.** Fictitious people and data, written to show what a real `semantic-harvest` interview looks like. The skill asked the questions from `questions.json` (generated by `sl harvest analyse`), in batches, proposing a guess wherever the parse or the probes gave one. The structured result is `answers.yaml`, which `sl harvest draft` turns into draft definitions.

- **Query:** `queries/core_analytics_pilot.oracle.sql` (legacy warehouse, Oracle dialect)
- **Interviewee:** avery.lane (core-analytics, author of the query)
- **Date:** 2026-10-03, about 40 minutes

---

**Agent:** I parsed your query. It reads one table in a CTE called `active`, joins stores and customers, and counts distinct customers by region, store and first-purchase month for the 2026-08-31 snapshot. Three questions first:
1. What business question does it answer, and who reads the result?
2. `ACCOUNT_STATUS = 'A'`: in the data I see A 88%, C 8%, P 3%. My guess is that A is open, C is closed and P is pending. Right?
3. `IS_STAFF = 0` excludes 2% of rows. Employee accounts?

**Avery:** It's the "customer base" page of the monthly management pack: how many active customers each store has, split by the month they first bought. Store managers and the regional leads read it. Status: A is an open account, C is closed, P is pending verification. Pending accounts can't buy yet, so they're not customers for this page. And yes, staff accounts are employees testing the app or buying with a discount. We always exclude them; they distort the small stores.

**Agent:** Next batch:
4. `LAST_PURCHASE_DATE >= ADD_MONTHS(SNAPSHOT_DATE, -12)`: is "bought in the last 12 months" the definition of *active*?
5. Is the CTE `active` a named concept? I'd call it *active customer*, one row per customer per month-end snapshot.
6. Both joins look many-to-one (the probes found store and customer ids unique on the right side). Can a customer exist without a home store?

**Avery:** Twelve months is the official window. The leadership team agreed on it two years ago, and finance uses the same one. The CTE is exactly "active customer", but please don't call it just "customer": marketing counts differently, and there's also the plain "has an account" count the onboarding team uses. A home store is always assigned at signup, so there are no gaps.

**Agent:** Last batch:
7. What is one row of the output? I read region × store × first-purchase month.
8. Which published figure should the total match?
9. What breaks this, or what have people got wrong before?
10. Who owns the tables, and who's the steward?
11. What do people change when they reuse it?

**Avery:** One row is a store and a join month, with region as a roll-up. The total has to match the customer-base headline in the pack: 446 for August, 449 for September. What goes wrong: people use the signup month instead of the first-purchase month ("joined" means first purchase here), and people forget the snapshot date and sum across months, which counts every customer twelve times. I'm the steward for this. The customer tables belong to core-analytics, and stores are ours too. People change the month, and sometimes filter one region.

**Agent:** Summary: one entity (*active customer*), one metric (customer count, never summed across months), two dimensions (*home store*, with store, district and region levels, and *join month* meaning the first purchase), two relationships from the joins, the query itself as a template with the period as a parameter, and the management-pack headline as an anchor. I'll also note that "customer" needs disambiguating. One open question: should pending accounts ever count? You said no for this page.

**Avery:** Correct. Pending never counts as active.
